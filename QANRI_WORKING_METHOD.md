# QANRI Website — Working Method Handoff

## Separation rule

QANRI is a new software product and must remain completely separate from URUTECH Elevators and Pergolith. Do not copy their brand language, claims, colors, product names, legal information, images, or industry assumptions. Transfer only the proven working method described below.

## Confirmed starting facts

- Brand and domain: `qanri.com`
- Product type: field tracking / field operations system
- Product also has an Apple application
- The website must present QANRI as a software company and a credible field operations product.
- Do not invent app features, integrations, customer counts, pricing, security certifications, App Store ratings, deployments, clients, partners, or performance claims.

## Working method to preserve

1. Preserve source truth
   - Separate confirmed facts, working assumptions, decisions, and open questions.
   - Never convert an assumption into published marketing copy.
   - Keep original logos, screenshots, product exports, and supplied files unchanged in a `references` area.

2. Inspect before designing
   - Review the actual iPhone/iPad application, screenshots, current domain state, logo, and any existing material before choosing the visual system.
   - Understand the real user, field workflow, buyer, daily pain, and purchasing decision.

3. Build a master brief
   - Define positioning, audiences, jobs-to-be-done, core workflow, feature hierarchy, trust requirements, conversion path, tone, content boundaries, and launch state.
   - Record unresolved items explicitly instead of filling gaps with generic SaaS claims.

4. Research with purpose
   - Study relevant field-service, construction-operations, workforce, inspection, maintenance, and project-tracking products.
   - Extract presentation logic and buyer expectations; do not copy brand expression or proprietary language.
   - Prefer primary sources for technical or platform claims.

5. Product architecture before pages
   - Establish the product story, key modules, workflow, data objects, mobile experience, reporting, roles, and integrations only from verified product behavior.
   - Map each page to a buyer question and a conversion goal.

6. Design system discipline
   - Create centralized typography, spacing, color, motion, border, card, button, and responsive rules.
   - Use restrained interaction, purposeful imagery, consistent hover behavior, and a clear hierarchy.
   - Avoid excessive empty space, oversized headings, decorative noise, and repetitive card grids.

7. Real product visuals first
   - Prefer real application screenshots, device mockups based on those screenshots, real field media, and verified interface flows.
   - Clearly label conceptual visuals when necessary.
   - Do not fabricate customer dashboards or operational proof that could be mistaken for a live deployment.

8. Content quality
   - Write specific product language around field outcomes, workflow clarity, accountability, evidence, and decision-making.
   - Avoid empty phrases such as “all-in-one,” “revolutionary,” “seamless,” or “AI-powered” unless precisely supported.
   - Maintain a consistent terminology dictionary across the website, app, App Store, documents, and future sales material.

9. Trust and commercial readiness
   - Separate a designed form from a connected form.
   - Do not claim live email delivery, demo booking, uploads, authentication, integrations, security compliance, or analytics until implemented and verified.
   - Track external requirements such as legal entity, support email, privacy policy, terms, App Store URL, data handling, security answers, real references, and pricing decisions.

10. Review from multiple viewpoints
    - Review as a world-class product designer.
    - Review as a software company executive.
    - Review as a field operations manager.
    - Review as an IT/security evaluator.
    - Review as a procurement decision-maker.
    - Convert findings into internal fixes versus external-input blockers.

11. Controlled iteration
    - Keep a prioritized task list and mark completed work.
    - Make coherent passes instead of random local changes.
    - Validate typography, density, navigation, mobile behavior, page titles, form honesty, media loading, route integrity, and production build after meaningful changes.

12. Publishing and backup
    - Keep local development, staging, and production clearly separated.
    - Never overwrite a working live version without an explicit publishing decision.
    - Preserve project identity and hosting configuration; do not create duplicate hosting projects accidentally.
    - Produce dated full backups before major publishing or structural changes.

## Initial discovery package

Request only what materially affects the first product brief:

- Original QANRI logo and brand files
- App Store link or TestFlight/access method
- Current application screenshots or screen recording
- One-sentence description in the founder's own words
- Actual users and paying buyer
- Verified feature/module list
- Supported Apple devices and minimum OS, if known
- Current legal company name and public contact details
- Privacy policy, terms, support URL, and data/security notes if they exist
- Current pricing or confirmation that pricing is undecided
- Existing customer references only with permission to publish

## First task in the new project

Create a dedicated QANRI project directory, copy this document into it as `QANRI_WORKING_METHOD.md`, initialize the website project, start the local preview, and then build the verified product brief before committing to final page content or visual claims.
